Caveats, read first:
- **β = 8 was chosen after seeing the real set.** The plan's β = 4 scored 7/23 there. The sweep {0, 2, 4, 8, 16} was
  in the plan, but the pick is in-sample. With 6 jaws the effective n is about 6–12. It must be frozen now and scored
  on the Teeth3DS test split.
- `gum` and `gum+colour` are the same meshes for geometry-only methods. That leaves 24 distinct geometry cases, and
  the walker solves 8 of them.
- Teeth3DS meshes are cleaned and remeshed, and the real click is an oracle (see the jitter results below).

## What I built

- `tools/pipeline-runner/src/natural/s2/harmonic.ts` is the whole method, in pure TS.
  - **Patch:** a BFS from the click inside a cylinder round the frame axis. Radius: template R + 2.5 mm. Height:
    from H + 4 mm below to 6 mm above the origin. A local CSR graph. Real patches have 9–17k vertices, synthetic
    43–87k.
  - **Seeds:** u = 1 within 0.4 × min(md, bl)/2 of the axis and within 0.35 H of the crown top. u = 0 on the patch
    rim. With P13 on, u = 0 also on the neighbours' crown tops: beyond md/2 + 1.5 mm along `frame.mesial`, within
    0.35 bl of the axis and 0.3 H of the top.
  - **Conductance:** `exp(−β · mean concavity / κ_ref) / edge length`. κ̄ is the bench curvature smoothed 2 more
    rounds; κ_ref is the 90th percentile of the patch's concavity. An optional colour factor
    `exp(−30 ‖ΔRGB‖²)` uses colour smoothed twice.
  - **Solver:** a Meyer watershed on the relief gives the initial guess, then Jacobi-PCG (tolerance 1e-4, at most
    2,000 iterations).
  - **Isoline:** marched through triangles and chained. The loop that winds round the axis is kept. The point on
    each crossed edge is the interpolated crossing (`raw`), the lower-u endpoint (`gum`) or the more concave endpoint
    (`concave`).
  - **Re-centring (option):** move the axis to the centroid of the u ≥ 0.5 region and solve again (at most 2 passes).
  - Fields are memoised per case, so ablations that change only the isovalue or the snap reuse one solve. Their ms
    figures are therefore meaningless.
- `tools/pipeline-runner/src/natural/methods/`: `harmonic` and its variants `-raw`, `-concave`, `-b0`, `-b2`, `-b4`,
  `-b16`, `-iso03`, `-iso07`, `-noneigh`, `-colour`, `-colourshift`, `-rc` and `-rc-raw`; plus `watershed` and its
  variants `-noneigh`, `-colour`, `-raw` and `-rc`. All are generated by `s2/gen-methods.mjs`.
- `tools/pipeline-runner/src/natural/s2/sectors.ts` is a scoring-side analysis. It reads the truth, which the methods
  never do. For each case it reports:
  - interproximal coverage and p95, using the critic's definition: projection on mesial beyond 0.6 of that side's
    extreme;
  - facial/lingual coverage and p95;
  - leaks (max > 1 mm);
  - with `--jitter 1.5`, the click moved by (1.5, 1.0) mm.
- Spec: `specs/2026-10-27-natural-margin-s2-harmonic.md`, added to `specs/README.md` and `specs/plan.md`.

## Ablations

- **β (crease weight):** 0: 0/23 · 2: 0/23 · 4: 7/23 · 8: 20/23 · 16: 20/23 real. Synthetic: 0, –, 12, 12, 12/32, with
  usable 0/16/20/21. My reading: the contrast has to reach about e^-8 at the 90th-percentile concavity before the drop
  concentrates on the crease. Above that it saturates, which makes β = 8–16 a stable plateau and not a knife-edge.
- **Isovalue:** see the table. 0.5 is best. The field is not a step on real posteriors (0.7 loses 4 posterior teeth).
- **Neighbour seeds (P13):** no measurable effect on the walker. Partial effect on the watershed (above).
  Teeth3DS's fused contacts did not need them, probably because the crease between neighbours is concave too and already low
  conductance. As a 2-label walker, P13 adds nothing here. A K-label argmax was not built.
- **Watershed (P4):** a 4× faster near-tie on real (20/23, 12 ms, median 0.08 mm), but it fails synthetic `bare`
  (2.8 mm), `hard` (1.18 mm) and half of `gum`. Plateaus of zero relief flood in arbitrary order, and one gap in the
  crease leaks. The walker's global averaging is what makes it robust.
- **Colour (synthetic `gum+colour` only; treat as an upper bound):**
  - Walker with colour conductance: 5/8, versus 6/8 geometry-only (raw: 8/8 without colour).
  - Colour shifted 0.2 mm along +x: 6/8.
  - So colour adds nothing to the walker, because geometry already finds this variant's line.
  - Watershed with colour relief: 4 → 5/8 correct, 5 → 8/8 usable. Colour works there as a leak guard.
  - Not testable on real (Teeth3DS has no colour).
- **Re-centring:**
  - Synthetic `hard` usable 4 → 7/8 (median 0.50 → 0.20 mm).
  - Jittered real 9 → 15/23.
  - Unjittered real unchanged (20/23).
  - It costs one more solve.

## Failure analysis

- **Real** (main; all 3 failures are usable):
  - 01F4JV8X 14: interproximal p95 0.72 mm.
  - SZQ66Y5A 31: lower central, fused contacts; interproximal coverage 72%, p95 1.28 mm.
  - V5U3CUGX 36: one facial/lingual stretch; facial/lingual coverage 81%, p95 1.06 mm. Raw fixes it.
  - Sectors over all 23 cases: interproximal coverage 99% and p95 0.19 mm; facial/lingual coverage 99% and p95
    0.24 mm. **Accuracy is balanced, and 2 of the 3 misses are interproximal.**
- **Synthetic `bare`** (0/8):
  - Truth coverage is fine: facial/lingual 100%, interproximal 82–100%.
  - The line, however, grows spurs. It climbs into the slot between crowns above the single gum-level contact, so
    the line is 2–7× the truth length, the mean is 0.2–0.4 mm and the max is 1.0–1.6 mm.
  - 11 bare (the wide upper central) fails outright (47%/59% coverage), probably from the seeds or the patch rim
    against the neighbour.
  - This variant has vertical walls with no gum cover, which is the least realistic case.
- **Synthetic `hard`** (0/8 correct): two causes.
  1. The off-centre click (1.5, 1) mm moves the seeds and the patch. Before re-centring, the facial/lingual coverage
     is 18–36% on 31, 14 and 13 hard. Re-centring fixes all but 31 hard (lower central, md 5, 20° lean, 34%
     coverage).
  2. The 2.6 mm papilla tips under Taubin 8 have a weak crease: interproximal p95 0.5–0.9 mm, mean 0.15–0.25 mm.
     That is usable but not correct.
- **Click jitter on real:**
  - Even after re-centring, 7 cases fail grossly: facial/lingual coverage about 50% and p95 about 5 mm, on
    EA8SBGX4 16/14, DNSRP767 16/11 and SZQ66Y5A 36/31.
  - Re-centring converges (residual shift < 0.4 mm), so the remaining cause is most likely the frame
    `estimateToothFrame` builds from the off-centre click: its occlusal and mesial axes, and so the seeds and
    cylinder. I did not have time to confirm this.
  - Fix to try next: derive the axis from the solved region (PCA of the u ≥ 0.5 vertices) as well as its centre, or
    use `jawOcclusal` for the cylinder.

## Time per tooth

- **Real resolution** (0.21 mm edges): 47 ms median (max 97 ms) on a 9–17k-vertex patch with 106–240 PCG iterations.
  Patch building takes 7–40 ms of that.
  - That includes an O(n) brute-force nearest-vertex search over the whole jaw, which the click's hit face would
    replace.
  - With re-centring: 69 ms.
- **Synthetic demo resolution** (0.024 × 0.08 mm grid): 315–470 ms median on 43–87k vertices; 1.2 s worst case, 2 s
  with re-centring. A real scan never has that density. If it must run at that density, decimate the patch or solve
  coarse-to-fine.
- PCG starts from the watershed labels. I did not measure a zero start for comparison.

## Scribble correction with the same solver

- Scribbles are just more Dirichlet seeds: a stroke on the tooth is u = 1, a stroke on the gum is u = 0.
- Re-solve warm-started from the current field. The change is local, so PCG should converge in a few tens of
  iterations. That is an estimate (not measured) of about 10–30 ms on a real patch, which is interactive at
  pointer-up.
- The isoline then moves only near the stroke. Elsewhere the field is pinned by the crease conductance.
- Two further tools fit the same solver:
  - **Here is the line:** a stroke can cut conductance along itself (×e^-8), which makes it a crease.
  - **Not here:** a stroke can raise conductance to erase a false crease (a fissure or a calculus ledge).
- Because β = 0 collapses, the user's strokes and the crease work together and do not fight. This is P15's kit
  without a second algorithm.

## What shipping it in the app's worker would take

- The code is already worker-shaped: typed arrays, the CSR `SurfaceGraph` and curvature the worker already holds
  (`analyzeSurface`), the frame from `estimateToothFrame`, and no DOM.
- About 450 lines move to `apps/web/src/geometry/naturalMargin.ts`.
- Work list, about **3 days**:
  1. Move the code, and start from the click's hit face (removes the O(n) scan).
  2. Output to the app's margin form: smooth the raw isoline with `smoothOnSurface` and hand it on as a polyline
     (or as anchors for `marginFromAnchors`).
  3. A confidence gate: a winding loop, length / template perimeter, the field contrast across the line, and the
     residual re-centring shift.
  4. One crown test on a synthetic natural tooth (pass), plus a failing case for the gate.
- Scribble correction: **+2 days**: seeds from strokes, a warm-started solve and the UI hook.
- Expected cost in the browser: about 50–100 ms per tooth on real scans. That assumes the worker's V8 runs as fast as
  Node did here, which I did not measure. No model, no network, $0.

## Recommendation

- **Adopt the crease-weighted walker as the natural-margin engine:** β = 8, raw isoline, one re-centring pass.
- **Ship scribble correction on the same solver.**
  - It is the best real-data result in this experiment (21/23 correct, 23/23 usable, median 0.10 mm, about 70 ms).
  - It is free.
  - It doubles as the human-correction tool.
- **Two conditions before trusting it:**
  1. Freeze the constants now and score the Teeth3DS test split once. β was picked in-sample, but β = 8 and 16 tie,
     so this is a plateau and not a tuned point.
  2. Make the frame robust to an off-centre click. Jitter costs 5–6 real cases even after re-centring, and that is
     the largest gap left.
- **Keep the watershed as a 12 ms pre-pass and initial guess, not as the method.** It ties on clean real meshes but
  leaks on synthetic.
- **Drop the neighbour seeds (P13) as a separate feature.** Keep them only as a leak guard.
- **Use colour, where it exists, as a gate or consensus cue.** As conductance it added nothing.
