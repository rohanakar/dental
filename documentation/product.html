Product & workflow

What a dental technician does in DentalCAD, which exocad features we replicate first and why, and which files go in and out.

In short
  • The MVP covers one job end to end: a single anatomic crown on a prepared premolar or molar, designed from intraoral scans and exported as a watertight STL for milling.
  • It follows exocad's step-by-step wizard: setup → scans → frame → margin → axis → parameters → proposal → adapt → export.
  • Inputs: STL and PLY scans. Output: binary STL plus a JSON sidecar (like exocad's .constructionInfo).
  • AI assist will offer a one-click draft crown, plain-language edits ("lower the occlusion 0.1 mm") and prescription intake. The technician always reviews and accepts.

Who it's for and what problem it solves

Dental labs and clinics design crowns in desktop CAD software such as exocad DentalCAD. DentalCAD (this project) brings the most-used part of that workflow to the browser:

  • Nothing to install, and cases are stored in the cloud.
  • Built from day one so AI can take over the routine steps, the way Dentbird does, while the technician keeps control.
Primary user
Dental lab technician (CAD designer)
Primary job
Design a single crown that seats on the prep, touches its neighbours and the opposing teeth correctly, is thick enough, and mills cleanly
Input
Intraoral scans (upper jaw, lower jaw; one of them has the prepared tooth), plus the order (tooth number, material, shade)
Output
A watertight crown STL for the CAM/milling software

Why a single posterior crown first (the 80:20)

Most of a lab's daily volume is single crowns and short bridges, on natural teeth or implants. Nearly every other restoration reuses the crown's building blocks (margin, insertion axis, inner fitting surface, outer anatomy, occlusion), so getting the crown path right covers the most real use and builds the foundation for everything else. Premolars and molars come first: their anatomy is more forgiving than front-tooth aesthetics, and their preps are usually "star-shaped" seen from above, which keeps the geometry robust.

The single-crown workflow, step by step

The app mirrors exocad's wizard: one step at a time, with Back/Next, and steps further down are recomputed when an earlier input changes. Each step has a manual path and, once the AI layer lands, a proposed result to accept or edit.

StepThe technician…The system…Status
1 · SetupPicks the tooth on an FDI chart (e.g. 36), the material (zirconia, lithium disilicate, PMMA) and the shade; or pastes the prescription.Loads the material's default parameters. With AI, it extracts the order from Rx text for confirmation.Day 2
2 · ScansDrops upper and lower STL/PLY files.Parses, cleans and welds them, builds a spatial index, warns on suspicious units, and shows them in the viewer.Built
3 · FrameConfirms the tooth's directions; clicks two neighbour contacts if auto-detection misses.Estimates the chewing direction, the front-to-back (mesial-distal) direction and the neighbour contact points.Day 2
4 · MarginClicks around the prep; the line follows the edge live; drags points to fix.Snaps the path along the prep's convex edge and smooths it.Day 2
5 · AxisAccepts or adjusts the insertion direction.Finds the direction with the least undercut, shows an undercut map, and warns if the margin is in undercut.Day 2
6 · ParamsReviews cement gap, spacer, thickness and contact targets.Builds the inner (intaglio) surface live and shows "unmillable" areas for the tool radius.Day 3
7 · ProposalReviews the proposed crown; moves, rotates and scales it in tooth terms (mesial, buccal, occlusal), with keyboard nudges.Places and deforms a library tooth onto the margin, scaled to the gap.Day 3–4

AI assist, from the technician's side

What general LLMs can't doClaude and OpenAI models are good at orchestration, extracting structure from prescriptions, and explaining problems. They cannot produce margin lines or crown surfaces at clinical precision (around 0.05 mm) from meshes. Deterministic geometry does that now, and specialised models will later. See AI assist.

Out of scope for the MVP

  • Bridges, implants, inlays/onlays/veneers, anterior teeth, dentures, splints
  • Virtual articulator and jaw motion; automatic bite alignment (scans are assumed to arrive in occlusion)
  • OBJ textures, DICOM, photos
  • Regulatory work and real patient data

After the MVP

  1. Bridges and implant crowns: the next biggest share of lab volume.
  2. Specialised models: tooth segmentation, then margin detection, then crown generation (Dentbird-style), then batch mode.
  3. Inlays/onlays/veneers, anteriors, model creation for printing.
  4. Clinical readiness: BAAs with vendors, de-identification, the medical-device regulatory path.
